# Earth-Loop / Spherical-World Integration Plan

> **For Hermes:** Do not implement this plan until the user explicitly authorizes the Earth-loop work.

**Goal:** Design a geographically coherent, finite Earth-like world in which longitude wraps at the antimeridian, latitude converges toward polar endpoints, and Latitude remains the sole authority for solar time, seasons, climate, and daylight.

**Architecture:** Implement this in layers instead of pretending that a Minecraft square can become a sphere with one coordinate modulo. First make the geographic coordinate system spherical and continuous (latitude, longitude, solar time, seasons). Then decide whether the physical terrain should merely have a safe east/west seam or whether chunk/entity/block access must be remapped across a genuinely spherical surface. The latter is a separate high-risk engine project.

**Tech Stack:** Fabric 1.20.1, Java 17, Fabric/Loom mixins, Minecraft world-border/chunk APIs, Latitude's existing `LatitudeCalendarMath` and `LatitudeSeasonBridge`.

---

## Current assumptions and non-goals

- Latitude stays authoritative for calendar, axial tilt, solar declination, local solar time, climate, precipitation, freezing, and effective daylight.
- The world remains manually launched by the user for runtime testing; no automatic Minecraft launch.
- No civil time-zone database or DST is assumed. The current baseline is longitude-derived local solar time.
- The first version must not rewrite stored sky-light data or corrupt existing chunks.
- The first version must not silently teleport arbitrary entities, structures, redstone, or block entities across a seam without an explicit topology decision.
- The “Earth loop” is not implemented by changing the world border alone. A border is a boundary, not a wrap.

## Topology choices that must be kept distinct

### Option A — Geographic longitude loop (recommended first)

Keep Minecraft's ordinary square chunk plane, but map X periodically to longitude with a stable width. At the west/east seam, longitude changes continuously from just below +180° to just above −180°. Solar time, date rollover, climate, sky direction, and debug output use the wrapped longitude.

- Low risk to terrain and saves.
- Gives a real antimeridian loop for geographic behavior.
- Does **not** make physical terrain, entities, projectiles, pathfinding, or block interaction wrap automatically.
- Suitable as the first validated milestone.

### Option B — Seam-safe physical world wrap

Keep a finite square world but intercept controlled entity/block-coordinate crossings and transfer them to the opposite X side. Add seam-aware chunk loading and targeted coordinate normalization for supported systems.

- More Earth-like to players travelling east/west.
- Requires careful handling for players, mobs, projectiles, vehicles, block entities, portals, commands, maps, structures, and chunk tickets.
- Must preserve a rollback path and be tested on a disposable fresh world.
- Do not implement until Option A is stable and the supported behavior list is explicit.

### Option C — True spherical terrain surface

Represent terrain on a sphere or cubed-sphere atlas, with chunk adjacency and movement remapped over multiple face seams and with pole convergence.

- Closest to an actual globe.
- Minecraft's native chunk grid, lighting, heightmaps, structures, pathfinding, redstone, rendering, and coordinate assumptions are planar; this is an engine-scale rewrite, not a normal Latitude feature.
- Keep as a research/design branch only until a prototype proves that chunk storage and rendering can support it.

## Open decision

Before implementation, choose whether “loop” means **longitude/geography only**, **physical east/west travel wrapping**, or a **true spherical terrain surface**. Do not conflate these three deliverables.

## Task 3: Finish physically correct solar direction

**Objective:** Separate the Sun's seasonal declination/north-south position from its daily/local-solar-time angle.

**Files:**
- Modify: `src/main/java/com/example/globe/world/LatitudeCalendarMath.java`
- Modify: `src/main/java/com/example/globe/world/LatitudeSeasonBridge.java`
- Modify: `src/main/java/com/example/globe/mixin/client/LunarWorldViewLatitudeSkyMixin.java`
- Create if required after mapping inspection: `src/main/java/com/example/globe/mixin/client/SkyRenderingLatitudeMixin.java`
- Modify: `src/main/resources/globe.mixins.json`

**Design:**

- Keep the existing daily hour angle for east/west motion.
- Use seasonal solar declination for north/south motion.
- Use the observer latitude plus declination to calculate elevation and azimuth; do not fake north/south movement by changing longitude.
- Use a visual-only atmospheric brightness function for sky color. Keep gameplay sky light and block light on their existing separate path.
- Apply rain, thunder, lightning, and dimension checks after the Latitude factor rather than replacing them accidentally.
- Inspect the exact 1.20.1 renderer method and refmap before adding a renderer mixin. A `getSkyAngle` hook alone changes daily phase but cannot fully place the Sun on the correct celestial declination.

**Verification:**

- At equinox, northern and southern locations at equal absolute latitude have mirrored solar paths.
- In northern summer, the Sun's declination is north-positive; in southern summer it is south-positive.
- Longitude changes local time/azimuth phase but not declination.
- At the same local solar time, a high-latitude daytime sky is visually dimmer than an equatorial sky when the solar elevation is lower, without making torches or the ground incorrectly dark.
- Build and mixin/refmap inspection pass.

### Practical bridge: the reflected-latitude cheat

If pole traversal is wanted before a face-aware sphere is feasible, use a folded latitude/longitude atlas rather than wrapping Z to the opposite pole. This is the deliberately simplified gameplay illusion:

- keep one canonical bounded latitude interval;
- keep X/longitude periodic;
- allow a player's logical meridional travel coordinate to overshoot a pole;
- reflect the overshoot back into the bounded interval;
- add half the circumference to longitude when a pole is crossed;
- transform the player's horizontal velocity and facing into the new tangent direction;
- apply the same mapping to supported passenger/vehicle state before resuming movement.

Conceptually, crossing the north boundary at `northLimit + overshoot` becomes `northLimit - overshoot` at the opposite longitude. Crossing the south boundary is the mirrored operation. This sends a player from the North Pole back down the other side of the northern hemisphere; it never sends them to the South Pole.

For a future pole-centered topology mode, use a separate logical meridional coordinate with the North Pole anchor at `z=0`, increasing `z` toward the equator and then the South Pole. At either exact pole, X is only an atlas representative because longitude is undefined; canonicalize the North Pole representative to `(x=0, z=0)` if that is the user-facing origin contract. Do not force every raw Minecraft block at that coordinate row to be the same geographic point until a dedicated polar-cap/atlas layer exists.

This is a useful cheat because it preserves the important geographic behavior without making Z a torus axis. It is not a perfect sphere:

- exact-pole longitude is mathematically undefined, so the chosen opposite-longitude representative is an atlas convention;
- an ordinary rectangular block grid does not shrink its east/west circumference toward the pole;
- arbitrary diagonal movement needs tangent-frame handling if it is to follow a spherical great-circle path rather than merely reflect a meridian;
- block/entity/chunk queries still need support around the artificial polar transition.

Implement this only as a bounded, player-focused prototype after the X-only loop is stable. Treat it as a separate topology mode with a hard fallback to the bounded map. Do not describe it as a true sphere until the face-aware experiment in Task 6 proves chunk adjacency and movement behavior.

## Time-zone interpretation to resolve before implementation

The phrase “time zones based on sideity” is ambiguous. The current Latitude implementation is **longitude-derived local solar time**, not civil time zones. Before adding a civil-time layer, clarify whether the desired behavior is:

1. continuous local solar time from longitude;
2. fixed-width longitude bands that display a zone offset;
3. named zones based on cities/regions; or
4. sidereal time tied to star rotation rather than solar time.

Do not introduce DST, a city database, or named civil zones until that choice is explicit.
